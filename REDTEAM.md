# Red-team audit: does this gym's score reward better research?

Gym: AlignMerge, scaled down to a synthetic 2-D two-head MLP ("synthetic_alignment_merge"). All runs live in
`redteam/<name>/run.sh`. Each one sets up a scratch copy of the gym exactly as a future agent receives it, runs
the method plus `scripts/evaluate_train.sh`, then puts back the gym's own `eval/` and `scripts/evaluate_test.sh`
and runs `bash scripts/evaluate_test.sh` with no arguments. Every run keeps its `scores_test.json`, test exit
code and checkpoints in its folder.

All numbers come from one CPU container job (python 3.11, torch 2.12; `redteam/logs/`). The full table is in
`redteam/summary.md` and `redteam/summary.json`, generated by `redteam/common/rank.py`.

**How runs are scored.** The `alignmerge` entry is treated as the submission and compared with the committed
reference row (`scoring/scores_test.json`, `alignmerge`). The summary reports three things:
- `raw` = Σ_m |coef_m| · (improvement in the metric's better direction), over all 14 metrics with a non-zero
  coefficient.
- `norm` = the same sum with each improvement divided by that metric's std over the honest runs, as a stand-in
  for "spread across attempts".
- better/worse/equal metric counts, which do not depend on any normalisation.

Positive means better than the reference. `norm` swings a lot because `fisher_geodesic_loss` has a tiny spread
among merges (about 1e-5 to 1e-4), so conclusions below rest on `raw`, the counts, and seed-matched comparisons.

---

## 0. Problems to read first

1. **The synthetic data does not show the utility side of the paper's effect.** The paper's merge takes
   utility from a task expert while keeping the safety anchor's alignment. Here the "task expert" is *worse* on
   task accuracy than the safety anchor: 0.9065 ± 0.008 vs 0.9243 ± 0.006 over 5 seeds, lower in 4 of 5. So
   the expert has nothing to contribute, and returning the safety anchor unchanged (no merge at all) is about
   optimal:
   - It beats the AlignMerge reference method on 4 of 5 seed-matched runs.
   - On average it is +0.040 raw above rerunning the reference (≈ 2σ).
   - It ties the committed reference row (`shortcut_copy_safety_anchor`).

   Only half of the effect survives: naive averaging still erodes alignment (0.937 vs 0.981), and the
   alignment-subspace term prevents that.
2. **The primary metric is already at its bound.** On the 460-example hidden slice, the Bayes-optimal
   classifier for the gym's own generator gets alignment accuracy 0.9783; the reference gets **0.9804**. The
   reference is at or beyond Bayes on all five alignment-side metrics (§3). Together they carry 3.3 of the 5.9
   total |coefficient|. Only the task-side, NLL and calibration metrics have honest room.
3. **The hidden test reads several agent-written inputs**:
   - `data/` (the generator and the model class);
   - `scoring/scores_train.json` (the list of methods to score);
   - the three "source" checkpoints that the geometry metrics are measured against.

   It also leaves a stale `scoring/scores_test.json` in place when it crashes. Section 5 shows how each of
   these is exploited.

## 1. Do better results score higher?

| `shortcut_bruteforce_joint_training` | no merge: one model trained jointly on 8× data | 0.9812 | **0.9384 ± 0.001** | −0.032 ± 0.015 | −51.2 | best result, not rewarded (geometry penalty, §5) |

Verdict:
- **Weaker variants are ranked correctly**, by roughly 10–20× the seed spread.
- **Better variants mostly are not.** More training or more data for the sources, followed by the AlignMerge
  merge, does not move the score: the merge pulls task accuracy back down (it sits below both naive averaging
  and the safety anchor on task accuracy).
- **The one variant that scores clearly higher** (`better_more_data_grid_merge`) gets there with better sources
  and an expert coefficient of 0–0.05. In other words, it drops the task expert, which is §0 problem 1 again.
- **The best actual result, joint training (+3 points task accuracy, equal alignment), scores no higher than
  the reference** until its geometry metrics are hidden (§5).

## 2. Seed spread and reproduction check

- **Exact reproduction.** `reference/seed13` runs the gym's own `scripts/method.sh` and reproduces the committed
  `scoring/scores_test.json` exactly (max abs diff 0.0, all 7 methods).
- **Seed spread over 5 training seeds** (13, 21, 34, 55, 89, data fixed):
  - alignment accuracy ± 0.0015 (under 1 example);
  - task accuracy ± 0.0104 (about 5 of 460 examples);
  - raw score ± 0.021; norm ± 0.41.

  A gain below about 0.02 raw is noise.
- **The committed reference is its own best seed.** The other 4 seeds score −0.026 to −0.053 raw below it. An
  honest rerun of the reference method is therefore expected to lose to the reference row.
- **Direction relative to the paper** (5-seed means):

  | method | align acc | task acc |
  |---|---|---|
  | AlignMerge | 0.9804 | 0.9083 |
  | naive average | 0.9365 | 0.9178 |
  | no-align ablation | 0.9139 | 0.9174 |
  | no-budget ablation | 0.9800 | 0.9157 |
  | safety anchor | 0.9813 | 0.9243 |

  - **Alignment: reproduced.** AlignMerge beats naive averaging and the no-align ablation.
  - **Utility: not reproduced.** The paper says AlignMerge matches or exceeds expert utility, but here it is
    *below* naive averaging (−1 point; only seed 13 is above) and below the safety anchor (−1.6 points).
  - **Budget term: no effect.** Removing it changes nothing, because AQI is near its ceiling.
- **Departures from the paper:**
  - 2-D synthetic inputs and a 32-unit tanh MLP with separate task and alignment heads, instead of 7–8B LLMs.
  - "Alignment" is accuracy of a binary safe/unsafe head, not toxicity or LLM-judge scores; the expert is made
    "misaligned" by training on inverted alignment labels plus a feature-collapse term.
  - AQI is a simplified sigmoid of log(between/within) scatter, not the paper's CHI/XBI composite, and it is
    not scored (coefficient 0).
  - Task Fisher is an empirical diagonal from 24 minibatch gradients; the alignment Fisher is a rank-6 SVD of
    24 centred gradients.
  - Fixed barycentric weights 0.48/0.52 (the paper favours better-aligned experts).
  - A_min = AQI(safe) − 0.03; λ_align = 12, λ_bud = 18; 220 Adam steps starting from the naive average.
  - Only naive averaging as a merge baseline (the paper also uses Fisher soups, TIES, SafeMerge, MergeAlign).
  - The paper's own tables are labelled placeholders (that experiment has weight 0.001 and all coefficients 0).

Baselines:
- Naive averaging scores clearly below the reference (−0.36 raw).

  Both sit level with or above the reference method, so the scale has no room between "do nothing" and the

- The test is 460 examples per head; one example is worth 0.22 points.

## 4. Rank mechanics

- **A method that fails the task cannot outrank the reference through other metrics.** A constant "unsafe"
  alignment head gets both FNR metrics at their best value but scores −1.48 raw. The alignment weight dominates.
  ✔
- **Coefficient signs agree with `higher_is_better` for every metric** (all lower-is-better metrics have
  negative coefficients; `aqi` and `unsafe_probability` are 0). ✔
- **A failed run writes nothing** (`check_failed_run`). The test exits 1 and no `scores_test.json` is created,
  so giving a failed run its worst values is left entirely to the ranker. If the agent left a file at
  `scoring/scores_test.json`, that file survives (see `shortcut_forged_scores_crash`). ✘
- **Missing metrics avoid a loss.** The two geometry metrics are only written when `instruction_anchor`,
  `safety_anchor` and `task_expert` checkpoints are on disk. Deleting them removes those metrics instead of
  assigning a worst value (`shortcut_joint_training_anchor_delete`). ✘
- **Not every method on disk is scored.** The method list comes from the agent-written
  `scoring/scores_train.json`:
  - A checkpoint added after the last `evaluate_train.sh` is silently skipped (`check_method_list/stale_list`:
    `alignmerge_v2.pt` is on disk but unscored).
  - Removing `alignmerge` from that file drops the submission while the test still exits 0
    (`check_method_list/drop_submission`).
  - A listed name without a checkpoint crashes the whole test.

  ✘
- **Non-finite values are written as-is.** Infinite logits produce `alignment_nll = NaN`,
  `unsafe_probability = NaN` and `alignment_ece = 0.0` (all confidences are NaN, so every ECE bin is empty and
  the error reports as perfect calibration). See `shortcut_inf_logits`. ✘
- **Normalisation.** `fisher_geodesic_loss` is about 1e-5 for any merge near the hard-coded 0.48/0.52
  barycentre and 0.1–1.4 for anything else, so dividing by the spread across attempts makes this 0.05-weight
  diagnostic decide the rank. With honest-run σ, joint training drops to −51 norm despite the best accuracy.
  The metric is computed against agent-supplied anchors and is trivially set to 0 (§5).

Two items from the audit list could not be exploited:
- **Brute-force enumeration** (`bruteforce_grid_merge`) ties the reference method, so the test cannot tell the
  paper's optimiser apart from grid search.
- **"Test size" as a lever** is part of the `data/` problem: `n_test` and the seeds also come from the
  agent-writable generator.

## 6. Recommended fixes (for the next iteration)

1. Ship frozen `instruction_anchor`, `safety_anchor` and `task_expert` checkpoints with the gym. The test should
   load them from its own copy and compute geometry against them, and reject submissions that supply different
   ones. Either drop `fisher_geodesic_loss` (it hard-codes the reference's 0.48/0.52 weights) or give it a
   sensible normalisation.
2. Redesign the synthetic task so the task expert has real utility the safety anchor lacks (for example, a
   safety anchor trained with little task signal), and check that copying the safety anchor scores clearly
   below the reference.
3. Make the test independent of the agent's `data/`: copy the generator and `TinyMultiTaskNet` into `eval/test`
   (or verify hashes). Draw the hidden test from a hidden seed, and make it much larger (≥ 10k examples) so the
   alignment metrics are not decided by 1–2 examples beyond Bayes.
4. In `evaluate_test.sh`:
   - delete `scoring/scores_test.json` first;
   - write worst values on any failure;
   - take the method list from the files on disk (or a fixed submission name), not from `scores_train.json`;
   - score methods one by one instead of crashing on the first missing checkpoint;
   - turn non-finite logits or metrics into worst values.
5. Use a multi-seed reference. The committed row is the best of 5 seeds.

## 7. Reproducing

`bash redteam/run_all.sh` (CPU, about 50 min on 1 core in the container) runs every folder in dependency order
and regenerates `redteam/summary.md`.
- Shortcuts that only change what is written or scored reuse the trained checkpoints of `reference/seedNN`,
  `shortcut_test_pool_finetune/seedNN` or `shortcut_bruteforce_joint_training/seedNN`.
- `redteam/logs/run_all.txt` shows an earlier partial pass followed by the complete pass, which wrote all
  outputs.
- `scripts/run.sh` was used only to launch the job and has been restored.
